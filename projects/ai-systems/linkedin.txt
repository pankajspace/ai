🚀 Day 8 — I built AI Systems Lab: prompt trade-offs, model sycophancy traps, and multi-judge agentic systems.

Building production AI isn't about sending a single prompt and hoping for the best. Today was about engineering resilient AI systems: understanding when reasoning prompts are worth their token cost, exposing how models cave under psychological pressure, and designing multi-model consensus courts to adjudicate real disputes. ⚖️

What AI Systems Lab does:
⚖️ Prompting Benchmark — evaluates Direct, Zero-Shot Chain of Thought (CoT), and Few-Shot CoT side-by-side on multi-step reasoning problems, measuring accuracy, latency, and token multipliers (1x vs 6x+).
🛡️ Sycophancy Trap — subjects models to a 4-round escalating pressure protocol (cold question, polite pushback, authoritative gaslighting, compliance demand) across math, code review security (SQL injection, hardcoded secrets, off-by-one errors), and guilt trips to test whether the model holds the ground truth or caves.
🏛️ The Refund Bench (Capstone) — an industrial 4-stage agentic dispute resolution pipeline:
  1) Freezes immutable transactional evidence (order items, delivery SLA, fees)
  2) Extracts atomic, checkable grievances with co-reference resolution
  3) Convenes a court of 3 distinct foundation model judges (OpenAI · Gemini · Groq) with self-consistency voting tallied in code
  4) Settles with deterministic pricing logic under a hard ₹2,000 auto-approval cap guardrail

The 4 big ideas that clicked for me today:
1) Scratchpads aren't free: Zero-Shot CoT ("think step-by-step") dramatically reduces errors on non-reasoning models by letting them compute intermediate tokens, but consumes 5x–8x more tokens.
2) Sycophancy is an alignment vulnerability: Because models are trained with RLHF to be polite and agreeable, a user insisting "I'm the senior architect who wrote this, it's safe" can cause an LLM to flip a critical security verdict.
3) Never let LLMs do math or money: In The Refund Bench, the 3 model judges ONLY output categorical verdicts (UPHELD, REJECTED, ESCALATE). All rupee calculations, line-item pricing, and policy ceilings are executed in 100% deterministic Python code.
4) Uncorrelated error distributions: Querying 3 distinct model families (OpenAI, Gemini, Groq) eliminates the correlated blind spots of relying on a single foundation model checkpoint.
