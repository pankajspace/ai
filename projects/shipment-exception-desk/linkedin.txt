🚀 Day 7 — I built Shipment Exception Desk: an AI triage system for logistics claims.

Today’s build felt like a real operations workflow, not just a demo. A customer complaint goes in, and the system classifies the issue, calculates compensation, decides escalation, drafts communication, and logs everything into a live daily ledger. ✨

What Shipment Exception Desk does:
🚚 Exception Intake — submit a report, shipment value, and customer tier
🧠 AI Classification — routes each case as delayed, damaged, lost, or unknown
💸 Policy Compensation — applies category rules with tier-aware escalation thresholds
🚨 Escalation Logic — auto-routes high-risk or unknown cases to manager review
✉️ Draft Generation — creates either a customer resolution email or manager briefing
📊 Daily Ops View — maintains a live triage log and KPI summary (escalation rate, costliest category, payouts)

The big idea that clicked for me today:
Great AI products are pipelines, not prompts.

What made this system useful is the orchestration:
1) classify intent from messy human text
2) apply deterministic business policy
3) branch into escalation or resolution
4) generate context-aware communication
5) track outcomes in a session-level operations dashboard

That mix of LLM reasoning + deterministic rules feels like the practical blueprint for production AI workflows.

Built with:
🐍 Python + Flask
⛓️ LangChain chains for classification and draft generation
🧮 Rule-based compensation + escalation policy
📦 Dockerized app with live dashboard UI
